How the model works

  1. Training data: 120 short messages, hand-labeled by the author — 60 scam/spam, 60 legitimate. No copied dataset, no real people's messages.
  2. Algorithm: Multinomial Naive Bayes with Laplace smoothing (α = 1). It learns P(word | scam) and P(word | ham) from the training messages.
  3. Prediction: For a new message it adds up log-probabilities per word and converts the scores with a softmax into an honest probability — the confidence you see is that probability, not a made-up percentage.
  4. Explanation: Every word's contribution (log P(word|scam) − log P(word|ham)) is shown, so you can see exactly which words pushed the decision.

Honest limits — read this

  • Small dataset: 120 messages is tiny. The model knows the patterns in these messages, not every scam in the world. A new scam wording it has never seen can slip through.
  • Not a security product: This is a learning demo. Never rely on it alone to judge a real message — if a message asks for money, OTPs, passwords or bank details, treat it as suspicious regardless of what any app says.
  • English only, SMS-style text: It was trained on short English messages. Long emails, other languages and heavily misspelled text will score poorly.
  • Confidence is a model probability, not a guarantee. A 90% score means "the model is 90% sure given its training", not "this is 90% a scam".

About

Scam SMS messages are a real social problem in India — fake KYC, parcel, lottery and loan messages trick people into sharing OTPs and bank details every day. SMS Guard is a beginner-friendly, transparent demonstration that a small, understandable ML model can learn to recognise those patterns — and that understanding why it flags something matters as much as the flag itself.
